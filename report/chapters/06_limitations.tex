Current limitations:
\begin{itemize}
\item computational cost remains high for full multi-pair, multi-seed runs;
\item final external scorer latency constrains rapid iteration;
\item conclusions depend on selected target pairs and may not generalize
to all polypharmacology regimes.
\end{itemize}

Methodological limitations:
\begin{itemize}
\item reward shaping and normalization choices can bias search trajectories;
\item docking-driven objectives remain proxy objectives rather than direct wet-lab endpoints.
\end{itemize}

Boltz-2 limitations:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Data leakage:} all selected targets appear in the Boltz-2 training set
(PDB cutoff 06/2023, $\sim$3M affinity records from ChEMBL). This does not invalidate
the comparison between generation modes (bias is identical across modes), but predicted
affinities may be optimistic in absolute terms. This must be noted when interpreting results.
\item \textbf{Cofactor incompatibility:} the Boltz-2 affinity module does not explicitly
model cofactors (Passaro et al. 2025, Rowan FAQ). For pairs COX1\_COX2 (heme in active site)
and AChE\_MAOB (covalent FAD in MAO-B), Boltz-2 affinity predictions should be interpreted
with caution. For MAO-B, \texttt{affinity\_probability\_binary} (binder vs decoy classifier)
is recommended over \texttt{affinity\_pred\_value}, as the binary classifier is less
sensitive to cofactor presence.
\end{itemize}

Docking score limitations:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Docking as a weak proxy:} PoseX benchmark (2025) shows that docking programs
(including AutoDock Vina and UniDock) exhibit near-random performance on several targets
in virtual screening. This is precisely why independent validation via Boltz-2 strengthens
the study---if the \texttt{double} mode advantage is confirmed by two independent methods,
the result is more convincing.
\item \textbf{UniDock GPU as reward backend:} FREED++ uses UniDock GPU
(\texttt{unidock\_gpu}) for docking-based reward computation. While UniDock GPU is
significantly faster than CPU-based alternatives (QuickVina2), the underlying scoring
function remains an approximation of binding free energy.
\end{itemize}

Surrogate mismatch limitations:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Training vs.\ evaluation metric:} FREED++ optimizes UniDock docking
scores $d_A, d_B$ (physics-based), while the primary evaluation metric is Boltz-2
affinity $a_A, a_B$ (learned surrogate). There is no guarantee that improving $d$
consistently improves $a$; the correlation is target-dependent. Per-target Spearman
$\rho(d_X, a_X)$ is computed as a sanity check; where $\rho \approx 0$, hit-rate
conclusions are flagged with additional caution.
\item \textbf{Boltz-2 calibration:} Boltz-2 affinity was trained on a mixture of
assay types (Ki, Kd, IC50, EC50, etc.) and should be interpreted as an IC50-like
\emph{ranking} surrogate, not a globally calibrated IC50 predictor. The fixed
threshold $T=0$ (1\,$\mu$M) is an empirical cutoff; target-specific calibration is
not performed. All hit-rate comparisons are \emph{relative} (double vs.\ single
under the same surrogate). Independent benchmarks show significant target-dependent
variability: PL-REX reports mean Pearson $r \approx 0.42$ (vs.\ 0.66 on the
canonical 4-target FEP+ subset from the original paper); DeepMirror per-target
evaluations range from $r = 0.26$ (cGAS) to $r = 0.81$ (Mpro). This motivates
the per-target Spearman $\rho(d_X, a_X)$ sanity check in our protocol.
\end{itemize}

Reward function limitations:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Reward function choice:} the choice of $R = -\max(d_A, d_B)$ (worst-case)
versus $R = -(d_A + d_B)$ (sum) influences the characteristics
of generated molecules. Different strategies introduce different biases toward balanced
vs.\ single-target-dominant solutions. The primary experiment uses worst-case only;
alternative scalarizations are evaluated offline as sensitivity analysis.
\end{itemize}

Validation limitations:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{No wet-lab validation:} all results are computational. Molecules optimized
for docking scores may not be real inhibitors. Experimental IC\textsubscript{50}
confirmation is beyond the scope of this workshop.
\end{itemize}

Statistical design limitations:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Shared targets across pairs:} several targets (AChE, BACE1, GSK3B) appear in
multiple pairs. Consequently, the 9~pairs (8~active) do not constitute 9~independent
observations. Primary statistical tests are conducted on the pre-registered
\emph{core set} (5~pairs, where target overlap is minimized), while auxiliary
pairs serve as within-target sensitivity analyses rather than additional
independent replicates.
\item \textbf{Core vs.\ auxiliary designation:} the core/auxiliary split was fixed
\emph{before} any generation runs to prevent cherry-picking. Results on auxiliary
pairs are reported for transparency but do not contribute to the primary hypothesis test.
\item \textbf{Bootstrap power at $m=5$ clusters:} the two-level bootstrap resamples
over 5~RL seeds (clusters) at level~1. With only 5~clusters the bootstrap distribution
is coarse-grained; confidence intervals and $p$-values should be interpreted with
caution. The primary evidence for the effect rests on \emph{effect size} (Cliff's
delta) and \emph{cross-pair consistency} ($\geq$3/5 core pairs), not on
$p$-values alone.
\end{itemize}

These limitations are tracked to prevent over-claiming and to guide future protocol upgrades.
